# π0.5: a Vision-Language-Action Model with Open-World Generalization
- Authors: Kevin Black, Noah Brown, James Darpinian, Karan Dhabalia, Danny Driess, et al. (Physical Intelligence). arXiv 22 Apr 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16054
- What: co-trained VLA (high-level subtask text + low-level actions) for cleaning/tidying homes not seen in training; ~400 h of mobile-manipulation data from ~100 homes; 97.6% of first-stage pre-training examples come from sources other than mobile manipulators. Scaling study over 3, 12, 22, 53, 82, 104 training locations.
- Robot: two mobile platforms, each with two 6-DoF arms + parallel-jaw grippers, a wheeled holonomic base and a torso lift (1D up/down on one, 2D up/down + forward/back on the other); 4 cameras; 18 or 19 DoF. No metric heights/footprint in the text (unverified).
- Eval: three real homes not in training (kitchens and bedrooms), 10 trials per task; rubric = points per step: Dishes in Sink (4 dishes, max 8), Items in Drawer (max 4: pick, open drawer, put in, close), Laundry in Basket (max 3, step 1 = navigate to and pick up clothing), Make the Bed (max 5). Per-task values only in figures (not extracted).
- Failure causes stated: unfamiliar drawer handles and cabinets physically hard to open; partial observability (arm occludes a spill); high-level distraction (repeatedly opening/closing a drawer); limited context/memory makes navigating between rooms and remembering object locations harder. Clutter, receptacle heights and base placement are not discussed.
- Relevance: the reference home VLA is evaluated room-by-room with nearby targets; articulated receptacles and multi-room search are its named weak points -> layouts should keep drawers/cabinets openable and task objects visible from the approach.
